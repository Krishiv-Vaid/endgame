First public release of endgame, a UCI chess engine written from scratch in C++20.

## What's in 1.0

- Fully legal bitboard move generation with magic bitboards; standard chess and Chess960. Verified by perft on 126 standard and 960 Chess960 positions at every published depth.
- Iterative-deepening PVS search with quiescence search, a transposition table, TT-move ordering, static exchange evaluation (ordering plus pruning), killer moves, delta pruning and mate distance pruning.
- Hand-crafted tapered evaluation (material, piece-square tables, mobility, pawn structure, king safety, bishop pair, rook files), with 511 middlegame/endgame weight pairs Texel-tuned on 4 million positions from endgame's own self-play.
- Time management that keeps a reserve at low clocks: a 1000-game sanity run ended with zero time losses, illegal moves or crashes.

## Strength

- The pre-1.0 baseline (search plus tuned evaluation) measured **2045 ± 26 on the CCRL Blitz scale**. That gauntlet was 988 games against Stash 11.0.1 – 17.0 at 10+0.1 on an Apple M4.
- Since then, 1.0 added TT-move ordering (+147 ± 28), SEE (+9 ± 6) and killer moves (+69 ± 19). Each gain was measured by a self-play SPRT against the previous build.
- Self-play gains overstate gains against other engines, so 1.0's own CCRL-scale rating hasn't been measured yet and isn't claimed here.

Linux and Windows builds are static. Checksums are in the `.sha256` files. The macOS binary isn't notarised, so you may need `xattr -d com.apple.quarantine <file>` before the first run.

## Known limitations

- Single-threaded search; `Threads` is fixed at 1.
- MultiPV 1 only, and no Syzygy tablebase support.
- No NNUE yet: it's being built and trained on endgame's own games for a later release.
